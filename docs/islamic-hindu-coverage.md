# Islamic and Hindu coverage: the Sufi, kalam, falsafa, fiqh and Hindu shelves

**Universal Wisdom Library** — 20 October 2026

This pass fills out the two thinnest areas of the catalogue. The Islamic shelf
had the Qur'an, the hadith collections and a handful of treatises; it now has the
major Sufi poets and prose writers, a creed from the kalam tradition, another
falsafa text and a larger fiqh shelf. The Hindu shelf had one excerpt from the
Gita; it now has the complete Gita, the principal Upanishads that are transcribed
in the Sacred Books of the East, the Yoga Sutras, the Brahma Sutras, the
Sarva-Darsana-Samgraha and the Ramayana.

Nothing here is copied into the corpus. Every work keeps a catalogue record, a
chapter outline and a passage index; the text is requested from the host when a
reader opens it. The existing streamed-reader architecture, provenance record and
rights display are reused unchanged.

### Islam — Kalam and falsafa (2 new works)

| Work | Translator, year | Host | Structure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| al-Ghazali, *A Short Creed* | Duncan Black Macdonald, 1903 | Wikisource | 1 chapter, 12 paragraphs |

The creed is a compact Ash'ari statement rather than a scholastic treatise; it
sits beside al-Ghazali's *Confessions* as the short and the long route into the
same theology. *Hayy ibn Yaqzan* adds the philosophical novel to the falsafa
shelf, next to Ibn Sina's psychology and the Averroes treatises.

### Islam — jurisprudence

The seven fiqh works are unchanged: the Muwatta is streamed from the Hadith API,
and al-Shafi'i's *Risala* and *Kitab al-Umm*, al-Sarakhsi's *Mabsut*, Sahnun's
*Mudawwana*, Ibn Qudama's *Mughni* and al-Marghinani's *Hidaya* remain catalogue
records. The research for this pass did not find a public-domain English fiqh
manual that can be served honestly:

- Khadduri's *Risala* (1961), Nyazee's *Bidayat al-Mujtahid* (1996) and the
  modern translations of al-Qayrawani's *Risala* are in copyright.
- Charles Hamilton's 1791/1870 *Hedaya* is public domain and digitised, but the
  available files are uncorrected OCR of a long nineteenth-century volume. The
  project's rule is not to present a machine-garbled scan as an edition, so it
  stays a catalogue record with that explanation.
- No public-domain English translation of al-Sarakhsi, Sahnun or Ibn Qudama was
  verified.

The shelf therefore states its position rather than filling the gap with a text
it cannot check. Adding the *Hedaya* would require a cleaned transcription or a
volunteer proofreading pass, not another importer.

## 2. Architecture changes

- `server/stream.ts`
  - `extractWikisourceSegments` accepts `{ splitBreaks: true }`. Some Wikisource
    poems (the Whinfield Masnavi) are one `<p>` per poem with a `<br>` at every
    line; the option keeps each line as its own segment so a verse can be cited
    by line. Page furniture (`layout 2`, the Khanda list) is filtered.
  - `plaintextLines` keeps one non-empty line per segment for verse editions.
  - Gutenberg chapters are trimmed at the licence block before splitting, so the
    last chapter no longer absorbs the Project Gutenberg licence text.
  - A chapter anchor can now match the first line of the file as well as a line
    after a newline.
- `scripts/dev/import-islamic-sources.ts` gained the six Sufi works, the creed,
  *Hayy ibn Yaqzan*, `splitBreaks` support and the Gutenberg `poem`/`endMarker`
  options. `--only=sufi` now includes the Gutenberg Sufi works.
- `scripts/dev/import-hindu-sources.ts` is new: five Gutenberg works and seven
  Upanishads from Wikisource, with a manifest at
  `data/imports/hindu-sources.json`.
- `server/stream.ts` gained an `archive` provider. It reads the item's metadata
  for the server that holds the text derivative, fetches the plain-text file and
  applies the same chapter splitter and verse-line mode as Gutenberg. The first
  use is FitzGerald's Attar; the source registry records item-level approval
  (`internet-archive-pd`) rather than approving the whole archive.
- `data/corpus/meta.json` gained eleven author records (Rumi, Hafiz, Saadi,
  Jami, Attar, al-Hujwiri, Ibn Tufail, Valmiki, Patanjali, Madhava, Badarayana),
  two schools (Vedanta, Yoga) and the item-level `internet-archive-pd` source.
  The Gita's theme, commentary, collection and relationship links were moved
  from the retired verse ranges to the paragraphs of the new streamed chapter 2
  (2.13 and 2.16).
- `package.json` gained `import:hindu`.
- No dependencies were added.

## 3. Rights and provenance

Every new edition names its translator, year, host and licence. The translations
are public domain in the United States:

- Wikisource marks the Sacred Books of the East volumes and the Whinfield
  Masnavi public domain.
- Project Gutenberg states public domain in the USA for every eBook used.
- The Internet Archive item for Attar carries the 1889 FitzGerald text with a
  proofed transcription that states it is public domain in the United States
  because it was published before 1923. The transcription also asks for
  attribution and non-commercial reuse; the edition credits sacred-texts.com
  and the registry records commercial use as not allowed. The registry approves
  that item individually (`internet-archive-pd`), not the archive as a whole.
- The Arnold Gita (1885), Bell Hafiz (1897), FitzGerald Jami (1856) and Attar
  (1889), Nicholson Hujwiri (1911), Ross Gulistan (1823), Syed Nawab Ali (1921),
  Brönnle (1904), Johnston (1912), Cowell–Gough (1882), Thibaut (1890) and
  Griffith (1870) are all nineteenth- or early twentieth-century translations.

The library does not relicense any text. It records the edition and links back to
the host in the reader's streamed-live notice. As with the rest of the streamed
shelf, availability depends on the host.

## 5. Limits

1. **Several editions are selections, not complete works.** Whinfield abridges
   the Masnavi; FitzGerald abridges Attar; Bell selects from Hafiz; Ross's
   Gulistan omits passages and the Gutenberg transcriber notes further
   omissions; Syed Nawab Ali selects eight chapters from the *Ihya*; Johnston's
   Yoga Sutras is an interpretive edition. The catalogue descriptions say so.
2. **The Upanishad set is not complete.** Wikisource has transcribed the
   Chandogya, Kena, Aitareya, Isha, Katha, Mundaka and Prashna Upanishads. The
   Brihadaranyaka, Taittiriya, Svetasvatara, Maitrayana and Kaushitaki pages are
   missing or untranscribed, so they are not catalogued with text. Adding them
   would require a verified transcription or a different host.
3. **No Sanskrit, Hebrew or Arabic original is shown for the new works.** The
   reader presents the public-domain English translation beside its provenance;
   the source-language column is empty because the hosts supply only English.
4. **The Ramayana edition ends with the sixth book.** The Project Gutenberg text
   of Griffith's translation does not include the Uttara Kanda; the note on the
   work says so.
5. **Fiqh is streamed in Arabic, not in English.** The 1870 Hamilton *Hedaya*
   remains unusable: the Internet Archive text corrupts words throughout
   ("puritication", "imitive", "proprictor"). A later pass read the Arabic
   directly from OpenITI and now streams al-Hidaya, al-Mabsut, al-Mudawwana,
   Bidayat al-Mujtahid and other manuals; see
   [`openiti-arabic.md`](openiti-arabic.md).
6. **The three tafsirs are Arabic only.** No public-domain English translation
   of al-Tabari, Ibn Kathir or al-Qurtubi was verified. The reader shows the
   Arabic commentary with the work, surah and ayah; it does not invent a
   translation. Quran.com's al-Tabari resource also omits commentary on a few
   ayahs, which the text marks in place.
7. **Poetry line breaks depend on the host's markup.** The Masnavi uses the
   Wikisource `<br>` lines; the Gutenberg poems use one line per line. If a host
   reforms a page, the reader reports that the served count no longer matches the
   index rather than silently reflowing the text.
8. **Streaming depends on the host.** Wikisource rate-limits imports and can be
   unavailable; Project Gutenberg is stable but not guaranteed; the Internet
   Archive can move an item to another server. The reader surfaces failures
   instead of substituting a local copy.
9. **Search covers the indexed shelves but not every streamed host.** SQLite
   FTS serves locally held texts; `stream_fts` serves OpenITI, the hadith
   collections, AlQuran Cloud, the Quran.com tafsirs and SuttaCentral; and
   Sefaria's API serves its Hebrew and Aramaic shelf. Project Gutenberg,
   Wikisource and Internet Archive works can still be browsed by tradition,
   work and chapter but are not yet in the local index. The acquisition
   register records the missing Upanishad and Ramayana pages with source leads.
10. **The Vedas, Mahabharata and Puranas are not in this pass.** The Rig Veda
   exists on Wikisource as 1,028 separate hymn pages, the Mahabharata
   transcription is explicitly incomplete, and the Puranas were located only as
   scan/OCR volumes. Importing them needs a page-crawl importer or a checked
   transcription, not another single-file adapter.

## 6. Reproduction

```bash
bun run import:islamic --only=sufi
bun run import:islamic --only=falsafa
bun run import:islamic --only=attar-bird-parliament
bun run import:hindu
bun run import:islamic-expansion
bun run import:openiti
bun run db:reset
bun run index:streams
bun test
bunx tsc --noEmit
bun run build
```

Manifests: `data/imports/islamic-sources.json` and
`data/imports/hindu-sources.json`. The source of truth for the streamed
addresses remains `data/registry/streams.json`.
